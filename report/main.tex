\subsection{K-Means and Bag-of-Visual-Words pipeline}

\subsubsection{Overview}

This pipeline explores how far a fully classical, non-neural detector and
classifier can go on the same task. The goal was not to beat YOLO/local-features
on accuracy, but to map the boundary of a bag-of-visual-words classifier when
the card is lying on a checked tablecloth, often partially covered by another
card. The detector is a K-Means segmentation followed by heavy morphological
operations and a strict geometric filter; the classifier is a BoW vocabulary built
from SIFT descriptors of the 40 reference scans. The pipeline is fully deterministic
(k-means is seeded at 42) and requires no training data, no GPU, and no learned model.

\subsubsection{Detector: K-Means plus local variance}

The detector has to solve one specific problem: the card face and the light
squares of the tablecloth have nearly the same colour, so a plain K-Means on
the raw frame merges them. The pipeline therefore applies, before any
clustering, a Gaussian blur ($81\times81$) whose kernel is larger than one
checkerboard period. When the kernel spans at least one full light/dark cycle,
every output pixel is an average of several light and dark squares, and the
tablecloth collapses to a mid-gray while the card face survives almost unchanged.

The blur alone is not enough when the kernel is not perfectly matched to the
pattern. A local variance map is therefore computed on the blurred grayscale
frame with two box filters ($\mathrm{Var} = E[I^2] - E[I]^2$). The card face,
being spatially uniform, has near-zero variance; the residual checkerboard has
high variance. A threshold on this map produces a \emph{uniform mask} that
removes the surviving fragments of the tablecloth before connected components
see them.

K-Means is then run on the blurred colour frame with $K=6$; the table is
modelled by the largest clusters, and the brightest foreground cluster is taken
as the card face. To recover fragmented regions caused by card figures, the mask
undergoes mass morphological operations (heavy dilation followed by erosion).
The largest blob is evaluated on five geometric properties, and its total combined
score must pass a strict global threshold ($\ge 2.1$) to ensure the blob is an actual
card and not a false positive like a player's hand or a shadow. The oriented
bounding box (\texttt{minAreaRect}) is computed and then padded mathematically
by 8\% to capture the physical card edges and provide a robust, context-rich crop
for the classifier.

\subsubsection{Recognition: Bag of Visual Words}

A vocabulary of $K=800$ visual words is built by running k-means over SIFT
descriptors extracted from the 40 reference cards and their augmented
variants. The augmentation covers rotations (including 180$^\circ$ for upside-down cards),
illumination changes, scaling, blur, and partial occlusions. The occlusions
are produced by cropping the card to a fraction of its area (40\%--90\% visible,
centered), which reproduces the actual geometry of the detector's output.

Each variant produces one reference histogram of size K. At query time, the
crop is aligned to the axes using the rotated rect, SIFT descriptors are extracted,
the query histogram is built, and the best match is chosen by chi-square distance
over all reference histograms in a single, highly efficient pass (since the
vocabulary is already rotation-aware).

\subsubsection{Pipeline integration}

\texttt{KMeansBriscolaProvider::find} scans up to 60 frames per round, calls
\texttt{findBBox} on each, and on the first successful detection classifies the
aligned crop. The provider implements \texttt{IBriscolaProvider}, so it can be
plugged into the existing \texttt{GameRunner} without changes to the rules or
the I/O.

The BoW classifier also replaces the built-in template matcher inside the
movement-pattern analyzer (\texttt{KMeansBowRoundAnalyzer}), via the \texttt{--bow} flag.
Because the frames selected by the movement analyzer contain multiple cards
(the briscola and the played cards), \texttt{findBBox} is guided by a
\textbf{Dynamic Temporal Mask}. By computing the absolute difference (\texttt{absdiff})
between a reference background frame (just before the player moves) and the
action frame, the pipeline isolates the exact region of new movement. K-Means
only searches inside this motion window, effortlessly ignoring the briscola and
any overlapping previously played cards. To ensure stability against transient
noise (e.g., a lingering hand), the pipeline uses a \textbf{Multi-frame Search Window},
scanning up to 5 consecutive frames and locking onto the first geometrically
perfect card.

\subsubsection{Results and limitations}

The briscola card was recovered correctly on all four provided games. The
played-card classification is around 40\% - 45\% across the four games.

The failure mode is systematic and intrinsic to the bag-of-words paradigm. A
card whose visible face shows fewer symbols than the real card (four denari
visible on a six-coins, five spade tips visible on a four-spades) is
misclassified toward the visually present rank, because the histogram loses
the descriptors of the covered symbols and the nearest reference becomes a
different card that happens to share the visible pattern. SIFT, which matches
descriptors locally and uses geometric verification, does not suffer from
this failure mode and is the recommended method when occlusion is expected.
